# Lab 1.2 – Exploratory vs. Scripted Testing

**Duration:** ~50–60 minutes  
**Prerequisites:** Node.js 22+, a modern browser, this repository cloned locally, Lab 1.1 completed  
**Modules:** 2 (QA Best Practices), 3 (Functional and Non-Functional Testing)

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lab you will be able to:
- Execute a scripted test checklist and record pass/fail results
- Run a timeboxed, charter-based exploratory testing session
- Apply negative testing and edge-case techniques (boundaries, invalid input, combinations)
- Classify defects as functional, usability, or negative/edge-case
- Explain what each approach is good at, and why teams use both

---

## Scenario

In Lab 1.1 you ranked **Shopping Cart (F4)** and **Discount Codes (F5)** as high-risk. A developer says the cart is "done and tested." They hand you a short scripted checklist that they say "covers everything."

Your job: run their checklist, then explore on your own, and report back on what each approach found.

---

## Background

| | Scripted Testing | Exploratory Testing |
|---|---|---|
| Tests designed | Before execution | During execution |
| Guided by | Step-by-step test cases with expected results | A **charter**: a mission and a timebox |
| Strengths | Repeatable, easy to delegate, good for regression | Finds unexpected bugs, adapts to what you learn |
| Weaknesses | Only checks what someone thought of in advance | Harder to repeat; depends on tester skill |
| Typical use | Smoke and regression checks, compliance | New features, high-risk areas, vague requirements |

### Edge-Case and Negative-Testing Techniques

| Technique | Ask yourself | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Boundary values** | What happens at, just below, and just above each limit? | Quantity 0, 1, 10, 11 |
| **Invalid input** | What if the input is the wrong type or empty? | Letters, blanks, negatives, decimals |
| **Variations** | Does it handle input that is "close enough"? | Upper/lower case, extra spaces |
| **Combinations** | What if two features interact? | Two discount codes; code + quantity change |
| **State & order** | What if I do things in a different order? | Apply code, *then* change cart; refresh page |
| **Consistency** | Does every page follow the same rule? | Out-of-stock on catalog vs. details page |

## Part 2: Exploratory Session (20 min)

Restart the server to get a clean cart. Then set a **20-minute timer** and explore.

**Charter:**

> Explore the **shopping cart and discount codes** using **boundary values, invalid input, and combinations** to discover problems with **pricing, totals, and the rules in F3, F4, F5, and F6.3**.

Guidelines:
1. Use the techniques table above. Pick one, try it, and follow anything that looks odd.
2. Keep short **notes** as you go: what you tried and what you saw. Notes are evidence.
3. When you find a bug, log it in the **Bugs Found** table with enough detail that someone else could reproduce it.
4. If you aren't sure whether something is a bug, log it under **Questions**. An unclear requirement is a finding too.
5. Stay on charter. If you notice something outside the cart (e.g., search), jot it down and come back to it later.

Ideas to get you started (you don't have to do them all):
- What quantities can you get into the cart? Try typing, not just the arrows.
- What codes does the app accept? What about `save15`, or `SAVE15 ` with a trailing space?
- Check [sample-app/README.md](../sample-app/README.md) for the list of codes. Do they all behave as their expiry dates say?
- How many codes can one order have?
- How low can you get the total?
- Are any books priced right at a business-rule threshold?
- Is "Out of Stock" enforced everywhere a book can be added?

When the timer ends, **stop**, even if you're mid-idea. Note where you stopped.

## Part 3: Classify and Compare (10 min)

1. In the **Bugs Found** table, set a **Type** for each bug:
   - **Functional:** the app does the wrong thing according to a requirement
   - **Usability:** it works, but is confusing or unfriendly to a real user
   - **Negative/Edge:** found by invalid input or a boundary value
2. For each bug, mark **Found by**: Scripted, Exploratory, or Both.
3. Fill in the **Comparison** section of your session sheet.

## Discussion Questions

1. The scripted checklist "covered" the cart, yet most bugs got past it. Why?
2. Step S6 asks only that "an error message is displayed." Did it pass? Should it have?
3. Which of your exploratory findings would you turn into **scripted regression tests**, and why those?
4. Which bugs trace back to the vague requirements you flagged in Lab 1.1?
5. Exploratory testing is hard to repeat. How did your notes help with that?
6. The quantity field's arrows stop at 10. Why didn't that prevent the quantity bugs?

---

## Key Takeaways

- Scripted tests only check what someone thought of in advance. They're **repeatable**, which makes them ideal for regression.
- Exploratory testing **adapts** as you learn and is best at finding the unexpected, especially in high-risk areas.
- Most bugs live at **boundaries**, in **invalid input**, and where **features interact**.
- A test can pass and still miss a problem if the expected result is too loose.
- Good teams use **both**: explore to discover, then script what matters so it stays fixed.

### Discussion Notes

- **Q1:** The checklist follows the happy path only. It has no boundaries, no invalid input, and no combinations.
- **Q2:** It passed as written. A better expected result: *"The message 'That code isn't valid' is displayed."* Vague expected results produce false passes.
- **Q3:** Good candidates: B1/B2 (boundaries), B4 (expiry), B5/B6 (money). These are high-risk, cheap to automate, and likely to regress.
- **Q4:** B8 traces to the "before or after discount / over vs. at $35" question. B3 traces to F5 not saying whether codes are case-sensitive. Students who asked those questions in Lab 1.1 were ahead of the bugs.
- **Q6:** The HTML `min`/`max` limits only affect the arrows. Typing bypasses them, and the server doesn't enforce the lower bound. **Client-side limits are not validation.**
- Expect most students to find 4–7 defects in 20 minutes. B8 and B9 are the least often found; use them to discuss charter scope and the consistency technique.
